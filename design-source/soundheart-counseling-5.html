Our NeuroRelational Belonging approach

Focused help for relationships and families

Everything we do flows from one idea. Connection is not something you find, it is something you practice. Our NeuroRelational Belonging approach helps calm the nervous system, so that people bound together in a marriage, a family, or after divorce can feel safe with each other again and find their way back.

Relationship counseling for crisis

When a relationship reaches a breaking point, weekly sessions can feel too slow. Intensive counseling gives couples concentrated, focused time to steady the crisis and find a way forward together.

Co-parenting after divorce

Separation ends a marriage, not a family. We help former partners learn to work together as parents, lowering the conflict children feel and building a calm, workable partnership around them.

Healing families & generational trauma

Some patterns are handed down long before anyone chooses them. We help disconnected and broken families reconnect, and interrupt the trauma that passes quietly from one generation to the next.

Also offering individual therapy for anxiety, depression, PTSD, ADHD, and neurodivergent and autism support.

The NeuroRelational model

A practice-first approach to change

The brain is a belonging organ. The patterns you fall into with the people you love were conditioned, not chosen, and that means they can be re-practiced. Think physical therapy, but for the nervous system.

01

The brain seeks belonging

We are wired to stay connected to the people who matter. Most of what looks like conflict is a nervous system trying to feel safe.

02

Patterns are conditioned

You did not choose the way you brace, shut down, or reach for reassurance. It was learned, which is exactly why it can change.

03

Change comes through practice

Insight alone rarely rewires anything. Repeated, deliberate practice does. We build that practice together, session by session.

You do not become what you believe. You become what you repeatedly practice, and what you practice, in the end, is belonging. Nawal Ibrahim Alhawsawi

Good questions

Frequently asked

Do you offer counseling in person or online?

Both. We provide in-person therapy and retreats in the Mat-Su Valley, and we also work with some people elsewhere online through services based on our NeuroRelational model.

What kind of counseling do you offer?

We focus on relationships and families: intensive counseling for couples in crisis, co-parenting support after divorce, and healing for disconnected families carrying generational trauma, all through the NeuroRelational approach.

Do you offer traditional counseling for other mental health conditions?

Yes. Alongside relationship and family work, we provide individual therapy for anxiety, depression, PTSD, ADHD, and neurodivergent support for autism and other conditions.

What therapy approaches and modalities do you use?

Our work is client-centered and trauma-informed. Depending on what you need, we draw on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT), Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), and EMDR.

What does an intensive look like?

Instead of spreading help across many short weekly sessions, an intensive gives you longer, focused time to work through a crisis. We tailor the length and format to what your relationship or family needs.

Can counseling help if my co-parent won't participate?

Yes. Even when the other parent isn't ready to join, individual co-parenting support can help you lower conflict, steady your own side of the relationship, and protect your children through the transition.

Do you work with whole families, not just couples?

Yes. Alongside couples and co-parenting work, we support families who have grown disconnected, helping them reconnect and interrupt the patterns passed down across generations.

What areas do you serve?

We are based in the Mat-Su Valley, where we offer in-person therapy and retreats. We also work with some people elsewhere online through services based on our NeuroRelational model.

What is the NeuroRelational approach?

A practice-first way of working with the nervous system. Rather than only talking about problems, we build small, repeatable practices that help your brain learn safety and connection, like physical therapy for how you relate.

How do I get started?

Book a consultation in a few simple steps. Have questions first? Complete our contact form and we'll help you take the first step.

Getting started

What to expect at your first session

Reaching out is the hardest step, so we keep the first one simple. Your first session is an unhurried conversation to understand what brought you in and what you are hoping for. There is no pressure to tell your whole story at once, and it is also your chance to see whether we are the right fit, because the relationship between you and your counselor matters more than anything else.

Sessions run about fifty minutes, in person in the Mat-Su Valley or online where available, and we will explain confidentiality and next steps clearly along the way.
