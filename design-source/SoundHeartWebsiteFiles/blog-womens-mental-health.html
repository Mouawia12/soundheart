Women's Mental Health: When You Are Holding Everyone but Yourself

By Nawal Ibrahim Alhawsawi, LPC · LMFT, SoundHeart Counseling · 5 min read

You are the one who remembers the appointments, senses the moods, carries the worry, and keeps everyone else afloat. But here is the question that so often goes unasked. Who holds you? Women carry an enormous, often invisible load, and their mental health frequently comes last. Putting yourself back on the list is not selfish. It is necessary.

The invisible load

Much of what women carry is unseen, the mental to-do list that never ends, the emotional labor of keeping everyone okay, the anticipating and managing that no one thanks you for. This load is real, it is exhausting, and carrying it silently for years quietly erodes well-being.

Unique pressures women face

  • Hormonal shifts across the lifespan, from cycles to matrescence to perimenopause, that affect mood in real ways.
  • Cultural messages to be endlessly giving, agreeable, and self-sacrificing.
  • A higher likelihood of having survived trauma, including experiences that began in childhood.
  • The pressure to do it all and make it look effortless.

How it shows up

Depleted women often do not look depleted. It shows up as high-functioning anxiety, irritability, resentment, exhaustion, numbness, or a quiet loss of self. Many women have spent so long tending others that they no longer know what they need, or feel guilty the moment they consider it.

Reclaiming yourself

Therapy offers something women rarely give themselves, a space that is entirely for you. A place to set down the load, hear your own voice again, learn to set boundaries without guilt, and be cared for instead of always caring. You cannot pour endlessly from an empty cup, and you were never meant to.

Common questions

What is the invisible load or mental load?

It is the unseen work of anticipating, planning, and managing a household and family's emotional needs, work that often falls to women and goes unrecognized. Carrying it silently for years can quietly erode mental health.

Why does women's mental health often come last?

Cultural expectations to be endlessly giving, the invisible load, hormonal shifts, and higher rates of trauma all combine so that many women put their own needs last and feel guilty prioritizing themselves.

Is it selfish to focus on my own mental health?

No. Caring for yourself is what makes it possible to care for others sustainably. You cannot pour endlessly from an empty cup, and tending your own well-being benefits everyone who depends on you.

When did you last put yourself on the list?
