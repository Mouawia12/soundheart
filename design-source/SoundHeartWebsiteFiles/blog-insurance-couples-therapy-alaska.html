Alaska & Getting Started

Does Insurance Cover Couples or Family Therapy in Alaska?

By Nawal Ibrahim Alhawsawi, LPC · LMFT, SoundHeart Counseling · 5 min read

Here's the honest answer most practices won't lead with: insurance usually covers therapy only when it's treating a diagnosed mental health condition in an individual. That means individual therapy is often covered, but couples and relationship work usually isn't. Knowing this up front saves you from surprise bills.

It feels counterintuitive, because couples therapy clearly helps people. But insurance operates on a medical model: it pays to treat a diagnosable condition in one person. Relationship distress on its own isn't classified as a medical diagnosis, so "couples therapy" or "marriage counseling" generally isn't a covered benefit.

What is usually covered

  • Individual therapy for a diagnosed condition, anxiety, depression, PTSD, and similar, is typically covered.
  • This includes conditions like seasonal depression and much trauma work.

What usually isn't

  • Couples and marriage counseling focused on the relationship itself.
  • Co-parenting and family sessions aimed at communication rather than treating one person's diagnosis.
  • Intensives and retreats.

How to check your own plan

Call the number on your card and ask specifically about "outpatient mental health" benefits and whether they cover CPT code 90847 for a client with a diagnosis. Ask about your deductible, copay, and whether you need to use in-network providers.

What SoundHeart offers

We're in the process of getting credentialed with major Alaska plans, so ask about current in-network status. Individual therapy can often be billed to insurance; couples, co-parenting, and intensive work is private-pay, and superbills are available on request. We'd always rather be clear about this than surprise you.

Common questions

Does insurance cover couples therapy?

Usually not on its own. Most plans only cover therapy that treats a diagnosed condition in an individual, and relationship distress isn't classified as a medical diagnosis, so couples and marriage counseling is generally private-pay.

Is individual therapy covered by insurance?

Typically yes, when it's treating a diagnosed condition such as anxiety, depression, or PTSD. This is the kind of care insurance most often covers.

What's a superbill?

A detailed receipt you can submit to your insurer for possible out-of-network reimbursement. We provide superbills on request for private-pay sessions.

Have questions about coverage?

We'll give you a straight answer about what your plan is likely to cover and what it won't, no surprises. Reach out and we'll help you plan.
