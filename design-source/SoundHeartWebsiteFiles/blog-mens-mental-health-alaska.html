Men's Mental Health in Alaska: Why Toughing It Out Is Not Working

By Nawal Ibrahim Alhawsawi, LPC · LMFT, SoundHeart Counseling · 6 min read

A lot of Alaskan men were raised on a simple rule. Handle it yourself, and do not complain. That rule gets you through some things. But when it comes to depression, anxiety, and the weight men carry from work, loss, and stress, toughing it out is not strength. It is a slow trap, and there is a better way out.

Depression in men often wears a disguise

Here is what most people, including a lot of men, do not know. In men, depression frequently does not look like sadness at all. It shows up as irritability and a short fuse, as numbness and going through the motions, as drinking more, working nonstop, or checking out in front of a screen. If the men in your life would never call it depression, that is exactly the point. It hides.

The Alaska pressure cooker

Alaska adds its own load. Long weeks on the slope or the boat, seasonal work with feast or famine income, isolation, dark winters, and a culture that prizes self reliance. Add the quiet expectation that a man should provide and not falter, and you get a lot of men carrying heavy things alone. None of that makes you weak. It makes you human and overloaded.

Signs it is time to get help

  • Anger and irritability that are not really about the thing you snapped at.
  • Feeling flat, numb, or disconnected from people you love.
  • Drinking, working, or scrolling to avoid what you feel.
  • Trouble sleeping, or exhaustion that rest does not fix.
  • A sense that you are just gutting it out, day after day.

Therapy that respects how you work

Good counseling for men is not about lying on a couch talking about your childhood for years. It is practical and direct. We work on what is actually happening, build real skills for handling stress and anger, and get to the root of the depression or trauma underneath. Plenty of the men we work with are surprised by how much lighter life gets. If you are ever in crisis, call or text 988 any time. Asking for help is not the opposite of being strong. It is part of it.

Common questions

What does depression look like in men?

In men, depression often shows up as anger and irritability, numbness, working or drinking to excess, or checking out, rather than obvious sadness. That disguise is part of why it so often goes unrecognized and untreated.

Why do men avoid therapy?

Many men were raised to handle things alone and see asking for help as weakness. But toughing it out tends to make things worse over time. Practical, direct counseling that respects how men work can change that.

What kind of therapy works for men?

Practical, solution focused work that builds real skills for stress and anger while addressing the depression or trauma underneath. It is direct and grounded, not years of vague talk.

Real strength includes asking for help.
