How Therapy Works & Our Approach

Our Approach to Relationships: The NeuroRelational Belonging Model

By Nawal Ibrahim Alhawsawi, LPC · LMFT, SoundHeart Counseling · 6 min read

At SoundHeart, we work from a simple but powerful idea. You do not become what you believe. You become what you repeatedly practice. And what you practice, in the end, is belonging. This is the heart of the NeuroRelational Belonging approach, and it changes how we understand both suffering and healing.

Connection is a practice, not a personality trait

We tend to think of being good at relationships as something you either are or are not. The NeuroRelational view says something more hopeful. The way you connect is a set of patterns your nervous system learned, mostly early, mostly without your say. And anything learned can be practiced into something new. Connection is less like a fixed trait and more like a skill your body can relearn.

Why the nervous system is the starting point

When we feel unsafe with the people we love, our bodies react before our thoughts catch up. We criticize, defend, shut down, or flee, not because we are bad partners or bad people, but because an old alarm is firing. Real change does not come from trying harder to think differently. It comes from helping the nervous system learn, through repeated experiences of safety, that connection is not dangerous. It is a little like physical therapy for how you relate.

Belonging as the goal of healing

Underneath most of what brings people to counseling, a couple in crisis, a family that has grown distant, an adult carrying old wounds, is a longing to belong somewhere safe. That is not weakness. It is the most human thing there is. And it is why healing happens best in connection, a truth we explore in our writing on post-traumatic growth.

How this shapes our work

Rather than only talking about problems, we build small, repeatable practices that help your brain and your relationships learn safety together. This approach runs through our couples work, our family and trauma work, our retreats, and the NeuroRelational Library of books. A sound heart is not one that never breaks. It is one that learns to mend.

Common questions

What is the NeuroRelational Belonging approach?

It is a practice-first way of working with relationships and the nervous system. It holds that how we connect is learned and can be relearned, and that healing happens through repeated experiences of safety and belonging.

How is it different from regular talk therapy?

Rather than only talking about problems, it builds small, repeatable practices that help the nervous system and your relationships learn safety together, similar to physical therapy for how you relate.

Who is this approach for?

It runs through all of our work, including couples in crisis, distant families, adults healing old wounds, and our retreats. Anyone longing for safer, more connected relationships can benefit.

Ready to practice a new way of connecting?

The NeuroRelational Belonging approach is at the heart of everything we do. Reach out to begin, in person in the Valley or by telehealth across Alaska.
