How Therapy Works & Our Approach

What Is Play Therapy? How Children Heal Through Play

By Nawal Ibrahim Alhawsawi, LPC · LMFT, SoundHeart Counseling · 5 min read

Adults process life by talking. Children process it by playing. Play is a child's natural language, and play therapy uses that language to help kids work through feelings and experiences they cannot yet put into words. For a young child, play is not just fun. It is how they make sense of the world.

Why play, not talk

Ask a distressed six year old how they feel and you will often get a shrug. Give them toys, a sand tray, art, or dolls, and their inner world comes out in the story they build. A trained play therapist reads that story, gently, and helps the child process fear, loss, anger, or confusion in a way that feels safe rather than overwhelming.

What play therapy helps with

  • Big life changes like divorce, a move, or a new sibling.
  • Grief and loss.
  • Anxiety, fears, and trouble managing big feelings.
  • Behavioral struggles at home or school.
  • Processing trauma or a frightening experience.

The parent is part of the healing

Play therapy is not something that happens to your child while you wait outside. Parents are essential partners. A good play therapist coaches you too, so the safety built in the playroom carries into your home. This often works alongside family therapy, because a child heals best inside a family that is healing with them.

When to consider it

If your child is struggling and words are not reaching them, play therapy may be the bridge. Our guide on when kids act out can help you sort out what your child needs and where to start.

Common questions

What is play therapy?

Play therapy is a form of counseling for children that uses play, their natural language, to help them process feelings and experiences they cannot yet express in words. A trained therapist guides the play to support healing.

What ages is play therapy for?

It is most often used with young children, roughly ages three to twelve, though the approach can be adapted. Older children and teens usually benefit from more verbal, skill-based approaches.

Are parents involved in play therapy?

Yes. Parents are essential partners. A good play therapist coaches parents so the safety built in the playroom carries into the home, often alongside family therapy.

Worried about your child?

Play therapy meets children where they are. Reach out and we will help your family find the right support, across Alaska.
