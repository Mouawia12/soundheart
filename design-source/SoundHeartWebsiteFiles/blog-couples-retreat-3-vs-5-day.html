3-Day vs. 5-Day Couples Retreat: Which One Does Your Relationship Need?

By Nawal Ibrahim Alhawsawi, LPC · LMFT, SoundHeart Counseling · 6 min read

A SoundHeart couples retreat gives your relationship something a weekly hour never can: uninterrupted time to step out of old patterns and practice new ones. The most common question we hear is which length to choose. The short version, the 3-day retreat is our standard for most couples, and the 5-day retreat is built for relationships recovering from a real crisis or a breach of trust.

Why a retreat at all?

Weekly therapy is valuable, but progress can stall when each session gets "reset" by the next week's argument before anything takes hold. A retreat removes the interruptions. With focused, guided time and nowhere else to be, couples get past the surface fight and reach the thing underneath, and they leave having actually practiced a different way of being together, not just talked about it. It's the same principle as an intensive, with more room to breathe.

The 3-Day Retreat, our standard

For most couples, three days is the right amount of time to make meaningful change. It's long enough to slow down, feel safe, and get real work done, and focused enough to fit into busy lives.

The 3-day retreat is well suited to couples who want to:

  • Reconnect after drifting apart, or after a long, depleting season.
  • Break out of the same recurring fight and learn new communication skills.
  • Strengthen a fundamentally solid relationship and invest in it before problems grow.
  • Prepare intentionally for a new chapter, marriage, a baby, blending families, retirement.

Think of the 3-day as a deep reset: enough time to shift the pattern, gentle enough to feel doable.

The 5-Day Retreat, for crisis and repair

Some situations need more time, not because the couple is failing, but because the work is deeper. When there's been a genuine crisis or a breach of trust, healing can't be rushed. The 5-day retreat gives that repair the room it requires.

The 5-day retreat is designed for couples facing:

  • Recovery after an affair or infidelity, where trust has to be rebuilt from the ground up.
  • A serious breach of trust, hidden finances, secrets, or broken agreements.
  • A relationship in acute crisis, where one or both partners are unsure they can stay.
  • Deep, long-standing wounds that keep resurfacing and need more than a brief reset.

Rebuilding trust has its own arc: it takes time for the hurt partner to feel safe asking hard questions, and time for the other partner to demonstrate real, consistent change. Two extra days often make the difference between starting that process and actually moving through its hardest early stretch.

How to choose

A simple way to think about it: if you're reconnecting and strengthening, the 3-day is likely your fit. If you're recovering from a betrayal or a crisis that has shaken the foundation, the 5-day gives the work the time it deserves. Still weighing whether to stay at all? Discernment counseling can bring clarity first.

Not sure? That's exactly what a short inquiry conversation is for. Tell us honestly where things stand, and we'll recommend the option that genuinely fits, never the longer one just because it's longer.

Common questions

What's the difference between the 3-day and 5-day couples retreat?

The 3-day retreat is our standard and suits most couples who want to reconnect, break recurring patterns, or strengthen a solid relationship. The 5-day retreat is designed for couples recovering from a crisis or a breach of trust, such as an affair, where rebuilding trust needs more time.

Which retreat is right after an affair?

In most cases the 5-day retreat. Recovering from infidelity means rebuilding trust from the ground up, which takes time for the hurt partner to feel safe and for the other partner to show consistent change. The extra days give that repair the room it needs.

Do I have to choose the longer retreat if we're in crisis?

Not necessarily. We'll talk with you about where things stand and recommend the option that genuinely fits, we'd never suggest the longer retreat simply because it's longer.
