Reconnecting After Deployment: Counseling for Military Couples Near JBER

By Nawal Ibrahim Alhawsawi, LPC · LMFT, SoundHeart Counseling · 6 min read

Deployment, reintegration, and the constant churn of military life test even the strongest marriages. Eagle River sits right between JBER and the mountains, home to many service members and their families, and the strain of coming back together after time apart is one of the most common, least-talked-about challenges they face.

Military couples navigate things most relationships never do: long separations, the fear and vigilance of deployment, frequent moves that uproot spouses' careers and kids' schools, and the hard work of becoming partners again after months apart. None of that means anything is wrong with your marriage. It means you're carrying an unusual load.

Why reintegration is so hard

While one partner was deployed, both of you changed. The one at home ran the household solo and built new routines; the one who deployed lived in a different world entirely. Coming back together isn't picking up where you left off, it's renegotiating who you both are now. That can look like distance, irritability, or feeling like strangers for a while.

Common pressure points

  • Reconnecting emotionally and physically after long separation.
  • Renegotiating roles and routines at home.
  • Deployment-related stress, hypervigilance, or trauma.
  • The isolation of a PCS move far from extended family, something Alaska amplifies.
  • Co-parenting through a parent's absences.

Support that fits military life

Telehealth across Alaska means sessions can continue through schedule changes and moves. For deployment-related trauma, EMDR is a leading treatment, and when reconnection has hardened into recurring conflict, learning to interrupt the same fight helps couples find their way back. TRICARE questions are welcome, reach out and we'll help you sort out coverage.

Common questions

Do you work with military couples near JBER?

Yes. We support service members and their families in Eagle River, Anchorage, and beyond, in person and by secure telehealth across Alaska, with deployment, reintegration, and the stresses of military life.

Why is reintegration after deployment so hard?

Both partners change during a long separation. Coming back together means renegotiating roles, routines, and identities rather than simply resuming where you left off, which can create distance or conflict for a while.

Does TRICARE cover counseling?

Coverage varies by plan and service. Reach out with your details and we'll help you understand your options, including which services are billable and which are private-pay.

Rebuilding after time apart?

We support military couples and families through deployment, reintegration, and moves, in person near the Valley and Anchorage, and by telehealth across Alaska.
