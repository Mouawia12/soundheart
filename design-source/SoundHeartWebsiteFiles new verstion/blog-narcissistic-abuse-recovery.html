Recovering After a Toxic or Emotionally Abusive Relationship

By Nawal Ibrahim Alhawsawi, LPC · LMFT, SoundHeart Counseling · 6 min read

If you have left a relationship that left you doubting your own memory, your judgment, even your grip on reality, please hear this. That confusion is not a character flaw or a sign you are crazy. It is a common injury from emotional abuse, and it heals. Reclaiming your sense of reality and self trust is absolutely possible.

A note on language. Not everyone difficult or hurtful has a personality disorder, and this article is not about diagnosing anyone. It is about the experience of surviving ongoing emotional abuse and manipulation, whatever the other person's label. If you are in danger, the National Domestic Violence Hotline is available at 1-800-799-7233, and in an emergency, call 911.

Why it messes with your reality

Emotional abuse often works through gaslighting, a slow erosion of your trust in your own perceptions. When someone repeatedly denies what happened, twists your words, and rewrites events, you begin to doubt yourself. Over time you can lose confidence in your own memory and judgment. That disorientation is the injury, and naming it is the beginning of healing.

Why it felt impossible to leave

People often ask why someone stays, or goes back. A big part of the answer is the trauma bond, a powerful attachment forged by cycles of cruelty and affection. The intermittent kindness, the hope that the good version will return, keeps you hooked in a way that has nothing to do with weakness. Understanding the trauma bond removes a lot of the self blame survivors carry.

How healing happens

  • Rebuilding trust in your own perceptions, slowly, with support that validates your reality.
  • Processing the trauma itself, where approaches like EMDR are especially effective.
  • Understanding the patterns, often rooted in earlier experiences, that made you vulnerable to the bond.
  • Reconnecting with your own needs, boundaries, and voice.

You are not broken, and you are not foolish. You survived something designed to disorient you. If you are supporting someone still in it, our guide on helping a friend in an abusive relationship may help.

Common questions

Why do I doubt my own memory after an abusive relationship?

Emotional abuse often involves gaslighting, a slow erosion of your trust in your own perceptions through denial and twisting of events. Losing confidence in your memory and judgment is a common injury from that, not a sign anything is wrong with you.

Why was it so hard to leave?

Trauma bonds form through cycles of cruelty and affection. The intermittent kindness and the hope that the good version will return create a powerful attachment that keeps people hooked, which has nothing to do with weakness.

Can you recover from emotional abuse?

Yes. Healing involves rebuilding trust in your own perceptions, processing the trauma with support and tools like EMDR, understanding the patterns involved, and reconnecting with your own voice and boundaries.

The confusion was not your fault.
