How Therapy Works & Our Approach

What Is ACT? Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, Explained

By Nawal Ibrahim Alhawsawi, LPC · LMFT, SoundHeart Counseling · 5 min read

Most of us spend a lot of energy trying to get rid of painful thoughts and feelings. ACT takes a surprising and freeing turn. It teaches you to stop fighting your inner experience and instead build a rich, meaningful life alongside it, guided by what you truly value.

The core idea

ACT, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, is built on a simple observation. The struggle to control or eliminate difficult thoughts and feelings often makes them stronger and shrinks our lives. ACT offers an alternative, making room for the hard stuff while committing to actions that matter. The aim is psychological flexibility, the ability to stay open, present, and pointed toward your values even when life is difficult.

What ACT teaches

  • Acceptance. Allowing feelings to be present without a constant war against them.
  • Defusion. Unhooking from your thoughts, seeing them as passing mental events rather than absolute truth.
  • Presence. Coming back to the here and now instead of living in worry or regret.
  • Values. Getting clear on what truly matters to you.
  • Committed action. Taking real steps toward that life, even with fear along for the ride.

Who it helps

ACT is effective for anxiety, depression, stress, chronic pain, and much more. It is especially freeing for people exhausted by the effort of trying to think their way out of every hard feeling. Rather than waiting to feel okay before living, you learn to live meaningfully now.

How we use it

We draw on ACT throughout our individual work, often alongside DBT skills and, when trauma is present, EMDR. It pairs beautifully with our NeuroRelational approach, because a values-driven life is almost always a connected one.

Common questions

What is ACT therapy?

ACT, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, helps you stop struggling against painful thoughts and feelings and instead build a meaningful life guided by your values. Its aim is psychological flexibility.

How is ACT different from CBT?

Traditional CBT often works to change or challenge unhelpful thoughts. ACT focuses less on changing thoughts and more on changing your relationship to them, making room for them while acting on your values.

What does ACT help with?

ACT is effective for anxiety, depression, stress, and chronic pain, and it especially helps people worn out by trying to think their way out of every difficult feeling.
